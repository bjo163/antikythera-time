# What the Antikythera Mechanism Actually Does

## Strongly supported by surviving evidence

### 1. Luni-solar calendar
The rear Metonic dial represents 235 lunar months over a 19-year cycle.

### 2. Lunar motion and phase
The surviving gearing models lunar motion; published work identifies a pin-and-slot mechanism corresponding to ancient lunar anomaly theory. A Moon display could show phase.

### 3. Solar position / calendrical position
The front display combines calendar and zodiac/ecliptic information and tracks the Sun.

### 4. Eclipse-cycle prediction
The Saros dial has 223 lunar-month cells and eclipse glyphs. The Exeligmos subcycle extends the recurrence framework.

### 5. Calendar synchronization
Metonic and Callippic cycles support long-term luni-solar calendar reckoning.

### 6. Games/Olympiad calendar
A subsidiary dial records Panhellenic games cycles.

## Supported by inscriptions but mechanically less certain

### 7. Planetary display
Inscriptions refer to the five planets known in antiquity. Modern researchers have proposed front-gear reconstructions that reproduce a planetary Cosmos, but only about one third of the original mechanism survives, so not every gear train is physically preserved.

## What it does NOT provide evidence for

- atomic seconds
- relativity
- TT/TDB/TCB
- the Hubble expansion rate
- cosmic microwave background observations
- the Big Bang epoch
- an exact age of the universe
- an absolute universal "now"

## Why it cannot determine the age of the universe by itself

The mechanism maps known astronomical cycles into mechanical ratios. It can extrapolate recurring Solar-System phenomena when supplied with the relevant cycle model.

The age of the universe is a different inverse problem. Modern estimates require observations of cosmic expansion, the oldest stellar populations, and especially cosmological model fits to data such as the cosmic microwave background.

Therefore:

```text
Antikythera:
known cycle -> geared ratio -> recurring astronomical state

Cosmic age:
cosmological observations -> physical model -> inferred time since early universe
```
